How to measure your DEGIRO portfolio performance

Reviewed 25 September 2026 by Sharpe

To measure your DEGIRO portfolio’s performance, separate changes in account value from deposits and withdrawals. Use monetary gains to understand how much you made, and time-weighted returns to compare investment performance over the same period.

Start with three different numbers

Portfolio value is a snapshot of what your holdings and included cash are worth. A deposit increases that value even if every investment price stays unchanged. A withdrawal reduces it without necessarily meaning you lost money.

Monetary gain measures the investment result after accounting for external cash flows. For a period with consistent account coverage:

Gain = ending value − starting value − deposits + withdrawals

For example, starting with €10,000 and ending with €16,000 after depositing €5,000 gives a €1,000 gain. Include cash consistently at both ends. Whether dividends, fees and taxes are already reflected depends on the values and reports you use; do not count the same item twice.

A percentage return answers a different question: how did the invested capital perform? Simply dividing that €1,000 gain by all the money you have ever deposited ignores when the money was invested.

The value rose by 60%, but the investment return was 10%. The €5,000 deposit explains the rest. This example assumes no other cash flows, fees or taxes.

What time-weighted return measures

Time-weighted return divides the timeline around external cash flows and compounds the returns of the subperiods. That reduces the influence of the size and timing of your deposits on the performance figure.

TWR = (1 + r₁) × (1 + r₂) × … − 1

Two subperiods returning +10% and −5% produce a combined +4.5%, because 1.10 × 0.95 − 1 = 0.045. They do not produce +5%. Practical calculations depend on available valuations and cash-flow timing.

A money-weighted return answers another useful question: what return did your own invested money earn, given the timing of your contributions? Neither measure replaces the other.

Read the correct DEGIRO figure

Do not confuse total account value with a position’s unrealised P&L. DEGIRO describes unrealised position P&L as a calculation based on break-even purchase prices, including currency movements but excluding items such as dividends and brokerage fees. That is not the same as a complete portfolio return.
